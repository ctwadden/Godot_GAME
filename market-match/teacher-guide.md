# Market Match / Data Choices

A 20–25 minute teaching simulation, provisionally timed; classroom testing is still needed. Based on the e-commerce and personalized-advertising discussion in the supplied chapter (4.2C, pp. 221–222). This is a focused extension, not coverage of currencies or the whole chapter.

## I can statements and evidence

- I can classify B2B, B2C and C2C using the actual buyer and seller: three checked transaction cards.
- I can distinguish seller, buyer, marketplace and payment provider: role checks and explanations.
- I can trace observed event → inferred preference → matching/ranking rule → advert → possible effect: profile inspection and written chain.
- I can compare contextual, consented interest and broad advertising with controlled conditions: two runs with the same product, audience, slots and seed.
- I can explain relevance, reach, missed customers and irrelevant impressions using figures: comparison response.
- I can explain how consent or deleting an input changes a result, without equating an inference with identity: changed-input experiment and P04.
- I can weigh a seller benefit against a consumer risk and explain how ads can fund a free service: trade-off and funding responses.
- I can explain possible exclusion from course/job opportunities and identify extra evidence needed before judging unfairness: transfer response.

## Suggested session

1. Expert demonstration, 5 minutes: use the seven paced steps, inspecting P04 and P09.
2. Student transaction practice, 3 minutes: classify the actual parties, not the platform.
3. Student controlled experiment, 8 minutes: predict, run broad and interest or contextual, inspect recipient decisions. Change consent or delete an event and rerun. Separate this from the policy comparison.
4. Explain and transfer, 5–9 minutes: download the local report and discuss.

No click score is a grade. Look for causal explanation, controlled comparisons and qualified conclusions. Ask students who was missed and why, not merely which policy had most clicks.

| Contextual | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 3 | 1 | 20 |
| Interest | 5 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 0 | 62 |

These invented results do not measure marketing effectiveness. Profiles, formulas, ranking queue and probabilities are authored teaching assumptions. The displayed rules and seed reproduce every result. Click probabilities are 55% for declared-interest matches and 12% otherwise; conditional purchase probability is 30%. A click does not guarantee purchase. Declared interests are an audit reference and an input to the separate outcome generator, never a matching input.

Interest needs consent and at least two observed events in the most frequent topic (ties use the displayed topic order). P04 browsed music as gift research but declares cycling: inference is wrong. Deleting E1 removes eligibility. P09 refuses targeting but is on a music page, so contextual remains available. Context itself can be sensitive.

Field counts measure matching input-field reads, including consent controls, not moral value, total storage or total processing. Zero does not mean zero privacy risk. Deletion removes the current fictional topic; restore regenerates the original synthetic input. Historical run snapshots remain for comparison and in exported reports. This is not a permanent-erasure service. Consent withdrawal stops future browsing reads, without deleting stored fictional events.

## Discussion connections

Identity: inferred profile versus actual interests. Power: platform controls ranking, visibility and data access. Values and ethics: consent, manipulation, funding and alternatives. Systems: separate buyer, seller, platform, payment provider and ad matching service. A platform can host several transaction types. The school purchaser is explicitly a fictional education business; do not generalize all school purchases as B2B.

Twenty synthetic profiles cannot establish real discrimination. Students would need eligible-population information, qualifications, exposure rates, decision rules, alternatives and evidence across groups and time. Advertising can pay for services through seller spending; it does not make every data collection acceptable.

## Access and privacy

Open index.html in a modern browser or use the published website. No installation, accounts, trackers, external scripts, real browsing history, camera or device-data APIs. The app keeps answers only in page memory until a learner downloads a text report. Do not enter names or personal data. Hosting providers may process ordinary web requests under their own policies; the game does not control that infrastructure. Reloading loses work. Reports are reviewed by the teacher, not automatically graded.
