# Nova / Global Launch: learning contract v3

Local draft for teacher review, 21 October 2026. Built and desk-checked; not yet tried with students or accepted as a complete unit. These are proposed local “I can” statements, not verbatim IB outcomes or registered assessment targets.


## Demo then student practice (current entry sequence)

The default entry now offers an authored expert demonstration, guided student practice, and the optional advanced campaign. The original full-campaign target map below still applies to the advanced mode; practice targets are a bounded subset, not a claim that every chapter target has been independently demonstrated.

First watch the eleven-step worked example (estimated 5–8 minutes). The coach runs actual transactions and explicitly names what the player controls versus what the game performs automatically. Follow money and parts separately, distinguish border receipt, assembly, truck dispatch and customer arrival, and explain why a bottleneck demands parts while a research goal may demand expertise. The expert briefly compares R&D arrangements; consecutive purchases are demonstrations, not a recommended optimal spending plan. After the first ten phones, backlog makes the next assembly batch thirty, which the narration explains.

Then start a fresh student company (estimated 10–20 minutes, untried with students). Eight tasks move from diagnosing screens, purchasing, receipt and customer delivery to one expertise choice, replenishment, a planned record fault and a ten-day 150-delivery challenge. Early stages expose only relevant controls. Current usable stock remains visible. Contextual advice is optional and logged; ask students to explain their own diagnosis before requesting it. Modes stop time while reading/writing. There is no Deliver action: day advancement moves automatic operations.

A slow first sea order is a real trade-off: it saves $1,200 but the starting ten screens cannot cover its lead time. Technical tests show a feasible air-first strategy reaching 160–170 deliveries in nine days; tested sea-first strategies reach 110 in ten days and receive a debrief rather than a passing grade. The target is not an assessment judgement. Discuss the delay and retry rather than treating a student's initially reasonable cost preference as a character failure.

The duplicate scan triggers after eighty deliveries and changes records, not physical screens. A protected $500 reserve and a review exit if cash is nevertheless too low prevent a forced audit from trapping a learner. Practice does not add random disasters before these dependencies are understood. The advanced campaign retains fixed and seeded disruption modes.

The end coach uses the student's actual stock, selected R&D approach, costs, deliveries, zero-assembly days and emissions. It also runs an illustrative policy for the same elapsed days, initial company and demand schedule: initial air screens, nearby sets when projected minimum stock falls below seventy, and existing-staff training from day four. The scan triggers independently in each run after eighty deliveries; the review states whether each actually reached it. This is one alternative, not a claim of optimality or a controlled causal effect of one isolated choice.

Students provide individual explanations of parts versus expertise, the limits of an audit, and a new provider/location transition. The exported report labels the mode and includes the actual practice prompts and choices. These responses, followed by an oral question or changed-case retry, provide stronger learning evidence than the delivery target. Staff salaries continue after product release; external R&D daily overhead ends at release. IP retention, contractor quality and labour conditions remain missing evidence, not calculated outcomes.

State: local model/UI tests and an independent pedagogical desk review informed corrections. Student comprehension and pacing remain untested. No new grading, LMS posting or whole-chapter attainment is implied.

## Intended understanding

Students should understand that digital coordination connects business activity across borders, but records and communication cannot remove physical dependencies. Provider control and geographic location are independent choices. Business recommendations require evidence about constraints and effects on people, rather than a universal “cheapest wins” rule.

## Actual learner brief

You run a Canadian phone company. Deliver 900 phones by day 30 while retaining at least 65 trust, 60 wellbeing and nonnegative cash. Supplier sales to Nova are B2B; Nova sells directly to consumers (B2C). Nova is both assembler/manufacturer and seller. No marketplace intermediary is modeled.

1. Work through Learn's seven steps. Place one real component order; observe the payment and unchanged physical stock. Advance a day and distinguish dispatch from customer receipt.
2. Run the campaign. Pause whenever you need to inspect figures. Hire, train, choose R&D arrangements, order parts, and respond to disruptions. The guide and glossary explanations remain available; there are no student logins.
3. Complete Learn's checks. Feedback supports retry and preserves attempts. Correct answers after feedback are practice evidence, not an independent assessment score.
4. In Report, write your own explanations in the three boxes. Compare two choices for the same goal, use two figures from daily records and the decision log, explain two stakeholder interests and one limit. Explain a disruption and transfer the reasoning to the school-printing case.
5. Save and export your report. Export is an anonymous local text file; the game displays its path. Hand it to your teacher using the teacher's existing submission method. No LMS connection or upload is built.

The deliverable is the exported report and a brief oral defence, not just the result screen. Group play may support discussion, but every student should provide an individual explanation or teacher conversation. Teacher controls assessment; no achievement or grade is posted.

## Learning arc and asset jobs

Estimated one-period sequence, to adjust after a classroom trial:

- 5 minutes: use the existing lesson's provider/location contrast and goods/data/money distinction. Model one example, then ask students to reconstruct the two axes.
- 7–10 minutes: guided introduction. Model one order; students predict and observe what changes. The guide can be skipped by experienced players and revisited.
- 20–25 minutes: campaign, with pause allowed. Students diagnose a bottleneck, compare a choice, and adapt to an event. The live map explains physical dependencies; stock/records explain information limits. Daily records retain evidence.
- 8–10 minutes: individual checks and report explanations. Feedback supports repair; ask for an independent explanation after retry.
- 3–5 minutes: changed school-printing case or short oral defence. Revisit the record-25/shelf-empty case next lesson without the game's labels.

Know: provider, location, sourcing, stock, capacity, TPS, lead time and stakeholders.
Explain: before → after → classification; goods/data/money; event → dependency → person → consequence.
Apply: compare equivalent goals, recommend conditionally, identify a missing fact, revise under changed conditions.

These timings are estimates, not classroom-tested claims. Students needing access support can use + Day only, read the guide at their own pace, and answer orally. All essential information is text as well as colour/icon. Keyboard tab navigation, buttons and scroll panels are available. Screen-reader support for the custom map has not been validated; Supply provides equivalent shipment names, modes, stages and remaining days in text. Audio is not required.

## What the simulation actually teaches, and its limits

Knowledge transfer, IP retention, contractor quality, accountability and contractor working conditions are questions for missing evidence. The game does not calculate them. Students should not infer them from nationality, ownership or a research rate.

Mechanically supported: global sourcing; provider/location distinctions and before/after transitions; comparing internal expertise and external services; transport/stock/assembly dependencies; data versus physical stock; record-driven alerts; tracking limits; stakeholder trade-offs. Lesson slide 15's two axes and slide 16's transitions now have manipulable equivalents.

TPS is represented by fictional order, receipt, stock and sale records, not a realistic enterprise implementation. The record alert is an explicit fictional inventory-service rule, not a claim that all TPS use this rule. The duplicate-scan event makes record quality consequential. Tracking gives an accurate one-day scenario warning for transport events; it is simplified event intelligence, not a prediction model. Lead times are deterministic except named event effects. Customs stages do not model actual laws, documents or duties.

The local factory button expands a stage Nova already owns. It does NOT establish vertical diversification. The Pro release improves an existing phone and is NOT automatically concentric diversification. Learn check 8 explicitly guards this error. The four diversification categories need the existing lesson examples and a separate transfer task; the game does not provide a full diversification system.

Recruitment uses relevant work samples and simplified immediate hiring. It does not teach automated screening bias, psychometric reliability, actual skills assessment or hiring fairness through a causal model. No lesson should claim otherwise.

Not taught through gameplay: gig/microwork economies; remote work infrastructure; targeted advertising; cashless exclusion; cryptocurrency/NFTs/e-trading; additive manufacturing; robot automation; job displacement; global customer markets/borderless selling. Current consumers are Canadian. These require separate lessons. No blanket whole-chapter coverage claim is warranted.

Fixed scenario: demand increases on days 6 and 21; storm day 9, inspection day 16, duplicate records day 22. Random mode samples three distinct events from storm, inspection, duplicate records, recall and extra demand, in bounded windows 7–10, 14–17, 21–24. Same seed reproduces the plan; economic decisions change the outcomes. Transport events affect relevant existing cargo and arrivals/orders in the active window. The game pauses after the affected day's processing, before the next decision; players cannot retrospectively undo that day's effect. A tracking purchase provides a one-day advance warning for transport events.

Storms are not the whole lesson. Operational/data/labour decisions matter too. Event effects are bounded and transparent, so students can explain them. These are scenario samples, not disaster probabilities or real country-risk comparisons. A higher score under a different seed is not a fair controlled comparison. Compare strategies with the same seed and starting state first.

Game abstractions: one flat product selling price, immediate recruitment/capacity installation, no interest/tax/exchange rates, one domestic customer market, abstract emissions units, no stockholding charge, no component redesign for the Pro. The fictional Portuguese site leases equipment that adds four research points/day when internal staff are ready, with its displayed setup/overhead. This is a site contract assumption, not a national skill comparison. Internal staff salaries continue when contracting R&D. External research replaces internal product research in that mode; it is not an additive free bonus. Part inventories are shared across models. Cash totals are not realistic business forecasts.

## Review and release state

Built: seven-step intro, two action gates, eight practice checks with explanatory feedback/retry, provider/location controls, real coastline basemap, ship/plane/truck/border icons, staged goods flows, seeded disruption modes, local written responses and report export.

Review: independent desk review identified the key misconceptions and recommended the eight targets before implementation. The final desk review checked the revised guide and mechanics, found post-game navigation and timed-writing issues, and prompted corrections. Local technical tests and visual captures are recorded in README. They establish code behavior and layout, not student comprehension.

Publishing the browser build does not connect an LMS/evidence registry or establish a student trial or formal teacher acceptance. Teacher review should decide whether to retain all eight local targets, what individual evidence is required, and whether timing fits the class. This does not block continued work already authorized on the game; it means the instructional blueprint is provisional.
