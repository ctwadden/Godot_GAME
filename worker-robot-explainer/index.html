What this model assumes—and how the money is calculated

Both scenarios operate for the selected working days and shift length. Every completed unit up to daily demand is sold immediately at the shared price. Production stops once daily demand is met; extra capacity does not create extra sales.

The worker is paid for the whole scheduled shift, including idle time. The robot’s supervisor is paid the selected scheduled hours each day, even when demand is low. Running costs are charged every working day. During a partial day, scheduled pay and running costs accrue proportionally.

Business profit = sales revenue − materials − paid labour − running costs − upfront purchase. Worker wages are an employee’s earnings and a business expense. The robot does not earn wages. Its purchase price is charged at day zero; no depreciation, borrowing, tax, resale value, breakdowns, learning period, rejects or other overheads are modelled.

Only whole units count. Catch-up is the first complete working day when cumulative robot-scenario profit reaches worker-scenario profit. A relative lead can still mean both businesses lose money.

The animations are AI-created illustrations of battery placement. The model’s output represents completed products; the clips are not measured cycle times or full manufacturing instructions. No audio or external AI grading is used.
