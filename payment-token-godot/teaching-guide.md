# Teaching notes — Payments & Tokens explained

Start with narration and let students watch one idea. Each has What happens, Why it happens and The impact. Pause or replay for discussion. The next idea waits for the learner; no configuration task precedes the explanation. Questions are optional, formative and unscored. Read without sound and the full TRANSCRIPT.md offer text alternatives. Canvas keyboard support is not full screen-reader accessibility.

Approximately seven minutes of voice across eleven ideas, plus pauses and optional questions. Actual classroom time and comprehension are untested. Ask for one individual explanation and a changed case after viewing, rather than treating a correct multiple-choice answer as proof of understanding.

Useful transfer prompts: Would using a card instead of cash change the currency? Does a flat phone prevent every digital payment? Does copying an image duplicate its token? Would a different explicit licence change commercial-use permission? Does a persistent token record guarantee that an external media host will remain available?

Source scope: supplied Economics.pdf, chapter 4.2C pp.223–225. Page 223 introduces cryptocurrency, NFTs, cashless society and microtransactions. Page 224 discusses random-reward purchases; its additive-manufacturing section is not taught here. Page 225 supplies the historical GameStop case, treated as an account to analyse rather than a prediction or endorsement of every claim. No textbook pages are redistributed.

Clarifications: not all digital payment is cryptocurrency; not all agreement mechanisms use proof of work; aliases do not guarantee anonymity; file possession, token control and licence rights are separate. The shared-ledger two-of-three example is an illustrative toy rule, not production consensus or real cryptographic security. Central balances omit fees and settlement complexity. Flowing dots illustrate a route, not repeated debits. All values, register identifiers and licence terms are fictional.

Primary sources previously checked for these distinctions:
- NIST IR 8202, Blockchain Technology Overview: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8202/final
- NIST distributed-ledger project: https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/privacy-enhancing-lw-distributed-ledger-technology
- Creative Commons FAQ on token ownership versus copyright/licensing: https://creativecommons.org/cc-and-nfts/

Narration is prerecorded synthetic speech, bundled with the project; it is not a live Gemini connection. No names, responses, voice recordings or accounts are collected. Hosting providers may process ordinary static-file requests. The lesson asks about fictional terms and historical sources, not current investment, tax or legal decisions.
