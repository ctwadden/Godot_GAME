# Revised brief — explanation before interaction

User instruction: replace the confusing dashboard with a simple Godot demonstration, visuals and narration, one idea at a time. Explicitly authorized design. This is an explanatory lesson with optional checks, not a management game.

Targets: distinguish payment route/currency/record; distinguish file possession/token control/licence permission; explain access, dependencies and stakeholder impacts. Chapter 4.2C pp.223–225. Additive manufacturing and trading mechanics are outside scope.

Sequence: one large visual, then What happens → Why it happens → Impact. The three voice clips progress within an idea; the next idea waits. Captions provide equivalent text. Recorded audio, pause, replay and silent reading require no live AI. Optional question appears after the explanation and can be skipped without penalty.

Sample: the book price remains 10 fictional credits while cash/card/account routes converge on it. Narration explains currency as the unit and method as the route. Impact: a shop can accept/refuse a method even for the same currency. Only then ask which changed if the buyer uses a card instead of cash.

Eleven short ideas split the earlier explanation into manageable scenes. No dashboard, access configuration, obligatory predictions, financial simulation, game score or secret lesson gates. The illustration and each question follow the explanation. All token/licence and amount examples are fictional. A broken link is illustrated locally, never fetched.

Technical completion: all 33 clips have real duration and caption hashes; pause affects voice and visual; auto-progress stops at the end of each idea; questions remain optional. Inspect representative rendered layouts and the exported web audio playback, then verify the downloadable source after import. A small student trial should check whether learners can explain the key distinction and its impact in a changed case; technical checks alone do not establish learning.
